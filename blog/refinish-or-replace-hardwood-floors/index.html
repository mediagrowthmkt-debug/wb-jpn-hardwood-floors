Refinish or Replace Your Hardwood Floors?

Solid boards? Refinishing usually wins. Here's how to tell which one your floor actually needs.

When floors look tired, people assume they need to be replaced. Often they don't. Here's how to tell the difference before you spend more than you need to.

When refinishing is the answer

If your boards are solid hardwood and structurally sound, refinishing sands away the old finish, scratches, and surface stains and brings the floor back to life. It costs far less than replacement and can be done several times over a floor's life.

  • Surface scratches, dullness, and worn finish
  • Light stains that sit in the finish, not the wood
  • Floors you simply want in a different color
  • Older solid hardwood with plenty of wear layer left

When replacement makes more sense

  • Deep water damage, rot, or warping and cupping
  • Boards sanded so many times the wear layer is gone
  • Severe pet or subfloor damage across the room
  • Engineered floors with a very thin top layer

How we help you decide

We assess the floor on site, check the wear layer and the subfloor, and tell you honestly which option pays off. Sometimes that means a repair and refinish; sometimes a section needs replacing first. Either way, you get a straight answer, not an upsell.

A good flooring company will talk you out of spending money you don't need to spend.
