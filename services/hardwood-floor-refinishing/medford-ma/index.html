Why refinish instead of replace?

Solid hardwood can usually be refinished several times over its life. If your Medford home has original Colonial-era or early-1900s solid floors, there is almost always plenty of wear layer left to sand. Refinishing keeps the character of the original wood, costs far less than a tear-out, and takes days instead of weeks. If a board is beyond saving, we repair or weave in a matching piece rather than replacing the whole floor.

Our refinishing process, step by step

  1. Free on-site assessment: we check the wear layer, subfloor, and any damage, and give you a documented estimate.
  2. Prep and protection: furniture is moved, the kitchen and adjacent rooms are sealed off, and vents are covered.
  3. Coarse-to-fine sanding: we sand down to fresh bare wood through the full grit sequence with dust-contained equipment.
  4. Repairs and prep: gaps are filled, squeaks are set, and any damaged boards are addressed before finishing.
  5. Stain test and color: if you are changing color, we test the stain on your own floor and confirm it with you first.
  6. Finish coats: two or three coats of durable oil-based or water-based finish, buffed between coats.
  7. Cure and walkthrough: we tell you exactly when to walk on it and when furniture can return, then walk the finished floor with you.

Signs your Medford floors are ready to refinish

Solid hardwood does not wear evenly, and older Medford homes often hide plenty of good wood under a tired finish. Here is when refinishing usually makes sense:

  • The finish looks dull, gray, or worn through in traffic paths and doorways
  • Surface scratches and scuffs you can feel but that have not reached deep into the wood
  • Light stains sitting in the finish rather than soaked into the board
  • You want to change the color of an otherwise sound floor
  • Boards are solid hardwood with wear layer left, not thin engineered veneer

Oil-based vs water-based finish for your Medford floor

The finish is what you actually walk on, and it decides how your refinished floor looks, smells during the job, and holds up for years. In Medford homes we install both oil-based and water-based finishes, and we walk you through the trade-offs before you decide.

Water-based finishes have low odor, dry fast, and keep the wood's natural, lighter tone. For busy family homes near Tufts or in West Medford where people want to get back to normal quickly, they are often the best fit, and today's commercial-grade water-based finishes are extremely durable.

Oil-based finishes take longer to cure and have more odor while they do, but they add warmth and a rich amber tone that many owners of older Colonial and Victorian homes love, and they are exceptionally tough. We help you weigh dry time, look, and durability against how your household actually lives.

Whichever you choose, we typically apply two to three coats with a buff between them, so the final surface is smooth, even, and built to take real foot traffic.

Choosing a stain color that suits an older home

Color is the most personal part of a refinishing job, and it is where the most regret happens when it is rushed. Stain reads completely differently depending on the wood species and the light in your specific room, so a chip from a catalog is never enough.

We test your chosen color on a freshly sanded section of your own floor, then look at it in daylight and again under evening light before we commit. That extra step is why Medford homeowners get the color they pictured instead of a surprise.

In historic Medford homes we often steer owners toward tones that complement original trim, stair rails, and built-ins, so the floor feels like it belongs to the house rather than fighting it. If you want to stay natural, a simple clear coat lets the character of century-old oak or pine speak for itself.

How often can a Medford floor be refinished?

One of the best things about solid hardwood is that it is not a one-time surface. A solid floor can typically be sanded and refinished several times across its life, because each refinish only removes a thin layer of wood.

That is why so many century-old Medford homes still have their original floors: previous owners refinished them a few times per generation instead of replacing them. When we assess your floor, one of the first things we check is how much wear layer is left, so we can tell you honestly whether you have one refinish left or many.

If your floor has been sanded down close to the tongue-and-groove, we will say so rather than take a job that damages the boards. In those cases we talk through repair, a light screen-and-recoat, or a plan to install new hardwood.

Screen-and-recoat vs a full refinish

Not every tired floor needs a full sand-to-bare-wood refinish. If your floor's finish is worn but the wood underneath is not scratched or stained, a screen-and-recoat, lightly abrading the existing finish and adding a fresh coat, can restore the shine for less money and less downtime.

A full refinish is the right call when there are real scratches into the wood, stains, or you want to change color. We look at your floor and recommend the lighter option when it will genuinely do the job, because the goal is the best result for your money, not the biggest invoice.

For many Medford rental units and busy family homes, a recoat every few years keeps floors looking cared-for and pushes the next full refinish years down the road.

Frequently asked questions

How much should it cost to refinish a hardwood floor in Medford?
Most standard refinishing runs about $3 to $5 per square foot, and a custom stain adds to that. The real number depends on your floor's size and condition, which is why we measure on site and give you a documented estimate with no surprises.
Can I stay in my house while the floors are refinished?
In most cases yes. The rooms being worked on are off-limits until the finish cures, but we protect the rest of the home and tell you exactly when each area is ready to walk on.
How long does it take to refinish 1,000 square feet?
A typical 1,000 square foot refinishing is a few days from sanding to final coat, plus cure time before furniture goes back. We give you a firm schedule in the estimate.
Is it cheaper to refinish or replace my floor?
If the boards are solid and sound, refinishing is far cheaper than replacing and keeps the original wood. We check the wear layer and subfloor on site and tell you honestly which one pays off.
Do I need to move all my furniture before refinishing?
We handle moving standard furniture as part of the job and tell you in advance what to clear yourself, like electronics, valuables, and closet contents. We protect everything that stays.
Will refinishing get rid of deep scratches and pet stains?
Surface scratches sand right out. Deep gouges and dark pet stains that soaked into the wood may need board repair or replacement, which we blend in. We tell you on site what will fully disappear and what needs a repair.
