Solid, engineered, or prefinished for a Medford home?

Many Medford homes have wood subfloors over joists, which is ideal for solid site-finished hardwood. If you are going over a concrete slab in a finished basement or an addition, engineered hardwood is usually the better build. Prefinished boards come sealed from the factory and are ready to walk on sooner; site-finished floors give a seamless look and any custom color. We help you choose based on your space, budget, and timeline.

Signs it is time for a new hardwood floor in Medford

Sometimes a floor is past refinishing and a fresh install is the smarter investment. Consider new hardwood when:

  • The existing floor has been sanded so many times the wear layer is gone
  • There is widespread water damage, warping, or subfloor rot
  • You are renovating, adding a room, or opening up a floor plan
  • You have worn carpet or tile over a subfloor and want real hardwood
  • You want a specific species, plank width, or pattern the old floor cannot give

Mistakes to avoid when installing hardwood in Medford

Installation problems almost always trace back to prep and product choice. Avoid these:

  • Skipping moisture testing over concrete or in a basement, which leads to cupping later
  • Laying solid hardwood over a slab where engineered is the correct build
  • No acclimation, so the floor gaps or buckles when the seasons change
  • No expansion gap at the walls, which traps the floor and causes it to lift
  • Choosing by price alone instead of the right species and finish for your traffic

Solid vs engineered hardwood for a Medford home

The single biggest installation decision is solid versus engineered hardwood, and the right answer depends on where in your Medford home the floor is going.

Solid hardwood is a single piece of wood through and through. It is the classic choice for the main living levels of the older homes in West Medford and Medford Square, it can be sanded and refinished many times over its life, and it develops the patina people love in a period house.

Engineered hardwood is a real wood wear layer over a stable plywood core. Because it moves less with humidity, it is the correct build over concrete slabs, in finished basements, and in additions, exactly the spots where solid hardwood would cup or gap. It still looks like real wood because it is real wood on top.

We assess your subfloor and the room's conditions and tell you honestly which build will last, rather than pushing whichever product is easiest for us.

Species, plank width, and layout

Once the build is chosen, the look comes from species, plank width, and layout. Red and white oak are the workhorses of New England floors, durable, widely available, and beautiful stained or natural. Maple, hickory, and walnut each bring a different grain and hardness.

Wider planks read modern and show off the grain; narrower strips match the original character of many Medford homes. We help you choose a width and layout that fits the age and style of your house, whether that is a classic straight lay or a statement pattern.

If you want a showpiece, we also install herringbone and chevron patterns, which turn a floor into the centerpiece of the room.

How we protect your home and get the moisture right

New England is hard on wood floors: humid summers, bone-dry heated winters. A floor installed without acclimation and proper gaps will telegraph every season change with cupping or gapping.

We deliver your wood ahead of time and let it acclimate to your home's humidity, moisture-test the subfloor (critical over concrete), and leave the correct expansion gap at the walls hidden under the baseboard. That is what keeps a Medford floor flat and quiet for decades.

Throughout the install we protect the rest of your home, contain dust, and keep clean transitions to adjacent rooms, then walk the finished floor with you before we call it done.

Prefinished vs site-finished installation

Beyond solid versus engineered, you also choose between prefinished boards and a site-finished floor, and the difference changes your timeline and your look.

Prefinished boards arrive sealed from the factory with the color and finish already on them. They go down fast and you can walk on them almost immediately, which is ideal when you need a room back quickly or you are installing during a larger renovation.

Site-finished (raw) hardwood is installed bare, then sanded, stained, and finished in place. It takes longer and adds cure time, but it gives a seamless, flush surface with no micro-bevels between boards and lets you pick any custom color. For the main living areas of a period Medford home, many owners prefer the site-finished look.

We install both and help you weigh speed against the finished look and feel for each room.

Transitions, stairs, and finishing details

A hardwood installation is only as good as its edges. Where your new floor meets tile, carpet, or a doorway, we build clean, low-profile transitions rather than slapping down a generic strip.

If your project includes stairs, we can match treads and risers to the new floor for a continuous look from level to level, a detail that matters in the open staircases common in Medford colonials and Victorians.

We finish with proper baseboard or shoe molding to hide the expansion gap, and we make sure vents, thresholds, and closet floors are handled so the whole install reads finished, not almost done.

What drives the cost of a hardwood installation

Installation is priced by the square foot, but several real factors move that number, and understanding them helps you budget with confidence instead of reacting to a surprise.

The biggest driver is the wood itself: a common red oak costs far less than wide-plank white oak, walnut, or a specialty species. Prefinished versus site-finished changes both material and labor, and a pattern like herringbone adds layout and cutting time.

The condition of your subfloor matters too. A flat, sound subfloor is ready to go; one that needs leveling, repair, or moisture mitigation over concrete adds prep before a single board is laid. Stairs, tricky transitions, and removing an old floor first all add labor.

We measure on site, look at your actual subfloor, and put every one of these lines in the estimate, so the price you approve is the price you pay, with nothing hidden until later.
