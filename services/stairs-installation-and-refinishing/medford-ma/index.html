Stair refinishing in Medford MA is some of the most demanding work in a home, and it is exactly where a good crew shows. Every tread and riser is a small, visible surface that takes hand sanding, careful staining, and a durable finish, and the whole staircase has to match the floor it connects. JPN is based in Medford, so we handle stair refinishing and new stair installation for homes throughout the city, from open colonial staircases to steep triple-decker stairs.

What stair refinishing and installation includes

  • Hand and machine sanding of treads, risers, stringers, and nosings
  • Squeak and loose-tread repair before finishing
  • Stain color matched to your existing floor or a new custom color
  • Two or three coats of durable, slip-conscious finish
  • New treads, risers, and railings when you are rebuilding a staircase
  • Full protection of the surrounding area and a clean handoff

Why stairs are harder than a floor

A floor is one continuous plane. A staircase is dozens of small surfaces at different angles, plus nosings and railings, and every one of them is seen up close. That is why so many refinishers avoid stairs or charge a premium for them. We do the hand work required to get crisp edges and an even color on every tread, so the finished staircase looks like a single, intentional piece rather than a patchwork.

Matching stairs to a Medford floor

In the older homes of Medford Square, West Medford, and Hillside, the staircase is often the first thing you see through the front door, and a mismatched stain stands out immediately. We match the color and sheen of your stairs to your existing hardwood floor, or refinish both together for a seamless look from the entry up.

Our stair process, step by step

  1. Free on-site assessment of treads, risers, railings, and any squeaks or loose steps, with a per-step estimate.
  2. Protection: the surrounding floor and walls are covered and the work area is sealed off.
  3. Repairs: loose treads are secured and squeaks addressed before any finishing.
  4. Sanding: treads, risers, nosings, and railings are sanded by hand and machine to bare wood.
  5. Color: stain is matched to your floor or your chosen color and tested first.
  6. Finish: two or three coats of durable finish, with attention to a safe, consistent surface.
  7. Walkthrough: we confirm the color match and clean up before handing the stairs back.

Signs your Medford stairs need attention

Stairs wear faster than floors because every step lands in the same place. Consider refinishing when:

  • Treads are worn through the finish in the center where everyone steps
  • The stair color no longer matches a refinished floor
  • Steps squeak or a tread feels loose underfoot
  • The finish is chipped or scuffed along nosings and edges
  • You refinished your floors and the stairs now look dated by comparison

What to expect during stair work

Most staircases take a few days including cure time between coats.

Because stairs are a main route through the home, we plan the work to keep you access wherever possible and tell you when each section is safe to use.

We protect the surrounding floor and walls, contain dust, and leave the entry clean.

Mistakes to avoid with stair refinishing in Medford

Stairs punish shortcuts more than any other surface. Watch for:

  • A refinisher who quotes stairs like flat floor square footage instead of per step
  • Skipping the squeak and loose-tread repair before finishing
  • A stain color that does not match the connected floor
  • A slick finish with no thought to a safe, consistent surface
  • Rushed edges and nosings that show sander marks up close

Refinishing vs rebuilding a staircase

Not every tired staircase needs rebuilding. If the treads and risers are solid hardwood and structurally sound, refinishing restores them for far less than replacement, the same logic as a floor.

Rebuilding makes sense when treads are cracked, badly worn past the point of sanding, or you are opening up a staircase as part of a renovation. In older Medford homes we often find good hardwood under old paint or carpet, and uncovering and refinishing it transforms an entry.

We assess the staircase on site and tell you honestly whether refinishing, new treads over the existing structure, or a full rebuild is the right call for your home and budget.

Carpeted stairs to hardwood

A common Medford project is converting carpeted stairs to finished hardwood. Sometimes there is solid wood under the carpet that just needs refinishing; sometimes the treads underneath were never meant to be seen and need new hardwood treads and risers installed.

We pull the carpet, assess what is underneath, and either refinish the existing wood or install new treads and risers matched to your floor. The result is a staircase that looks built for the home rather than covered up.

Converting to hardwood also removes a magnet for dust and allergens, a real benefit in the busy family homes near Tufts and throughout West Medford.

Safety and finish on stairs

Stairs are the one surface in the home where finish choice is partly about safety, not just looks. A finish that is too glossy and slick underfoot is a hazard, especially on a steep older staircase.

We use durable finishes appropriate for stair traffic and can discuss options that keep a consistent, confident footing while still matching the sheen of your floor.

Crisp, even nosings matter too: a well-finished nosing is easier to see and step on. The hand work we put into edges is as much about safe stairs as it is about how they look.
