Subfloor installation and leveling in Medford MA is the step nobody sees and every good floor depends on. Whatever goes on top, hardwood, engineered, laminate, or vinyl, is only as flat, quiet, and long-lasting as the base beneath it. In Medford's older homes, subfloors are often uneven, squeaky, or damaged after a century of settling, and getting the base right is the difference between a floor that lasts decades and one that fails early.

What subfloor prep and leveling includes

  • Inspection of the existing subfloor, joists, and any damage
  • Moisture testing, especially over concrete slabs
  • Leveling with self-leveling compound or shimming to a flat plane
  • Repair or replacement of damaged, rotted, or water-affected sections
  • Securing loose panels and eliminating squeaks from below the finish floor
  • A flat, sound, dry base ready for any floor you choose

Why the subfloor decides everything

A finished floor is only as good as what it sits on. A subfloor with dips and high spots causes flooring to flex, gap, and squeak; a damp subfloor causes cupping and failure; a loose or damaged subfloor causes movement and noise. Every one of the most common floor complaints traces back to a base that was not prepped. We fix the base first, so the floor on top performs the way it should.

Subfloors in older Medford homes

Many homes in Medford Square, Hillside, and South Medford have wood-plank or early plywood subfloors that have settled, dried, and shifted over a hundred New England winters. They are often out of level, squeaky, and sometimes water-damaged near radiators and bathrooms. We assess what is really there and level, repair, or reinforce it so a new floor has the flat, quiet, solid base these homes were not left with.

Our subfloor process, step by step

  1. Inspection of the subfloor, joists, and any damage, with moisture testing.
  2. Diagnosis: we identify dips, high spots, squeaks, and any rot or water damage.
  3. Repair: damaged or rotted sections are repaired or replaced, panels secured.
  4. Leveling: self-leveling compound, shimming, or sanding to a flat plane.
  5. Moisture: over slabs, the right mitigation and vapor management.
  6. Verification: we confirm the base is flat, sound, and dry before any floor goes on.

Signs your Medford subfloor needs work

The base often announces itself before the finish floor fails. Watch for:

  • Floors that feel bouncy, soft, or uneven underfoot
  • Persistent squeaks that move around as you walk
  • Visible dips, humps, or slopes across a room
  • Water damage or a musty smell near radiators, sinks, or the basement
  • A previous floor that cupped, gapped, or failed early

Self-leveling compound vs mechanical leveling

There is more than one way to flatten a subfloor, and the right approach depends on the surface and how far out of level it is. Choosing correctly is part of doing the base right.

Self-leveling compound is a pourable mix that flows into dips and cures to a flat, hard surface, ideal over concrete or a sound but uneven wood subfloor. Mechanical leveling, sanding down high spots or shimming and adding a new layer, suits situations where a pour is not appropriate or the framing itself needs adjustment.

We assess the subfloor, measure how far out of flat it is, and choose the method, or combination, that gives a truly flat plane for your specific base rather than a quick skim that leaves the problem underneath.

Moisture, the hidden killer of floors

Moisture is the most common cause of a floor failing from below, and it is invisible until the damage is done. Any wood or laminate floor installed over a damp subfloor or an untested slab is on borrowed time.

We moisture-test before installing, especially over concrete in Medford basements and additions, and use the right vapor barrier or mitigation when readings call for it. This is exactly the step that separates a floor that lasts from one that cups and buckles a year later.

If we find a moisture source, a leaking radiator, a damp slab, a bathroom that has been wicking water into the subfloor, we address the cause, not just the symptom, before any new floor goes down.

Fixing squeaks and bounce at the source

Squeaks and a bouncy, soft feel underfoot almost always come from the base: a subfloor panel moving against a joist, a loose fastener, or inadequate support in an older home.

The lasting fix is to secure the subfloor to the joists and reinforce any spots that flex, done before the finish floor goes on so it never has to be torn up later. Covering a squeak with a new floor just buries it, and it comes back.

In Medford's older homes we often reinforce and re-secure the base as part of the prep, so the new floor is not only flat but genuinely quiet and solid underfoot, the way a new floor should feel.

How flat does a subfloor really need to be?

Homeowners are often surprised how strict flooring flatness tolerances are. Most manufacturers specify the subfloor be flat within a small fraction of an inch over a set distance, and installing outside that spec can void the product warranty and cause the floor to fail.

For floating floors like laminate and vinyl plank, flatness is everything, dips cause flex and hollow spots, high spots cause rocking and cracking at the joints. For nailed hardwood, an out-of-flat base leads to squeaks and uneven wear. The tolerance is tighter than most people expect and than most handymen check.

We measure the subfloor against the flatness the specific product requires, not by eye, and level it to that standard. It is invisible work, and it is the single biggest reason a floor either lasts or fails.

Plywood, OSB, plank, and concrete subfloors

Medford homes have every kind of subfloor, and each needs a different approach. Knowing what you have is the first step to preparing it correctly.

Old homes often have diagonal wood-plank subfloors that have shifted and gapped over a century; these may need an added underlayment layer to create a smooth, continuous surface. Newer construction uses plywood or OSB, which is easier to work with but still needs to be flat, dry, and well-fastened. Basements and additions sit on concrete slabs, which demand moisture testing and often a leveling compound.

We identify your subfloor type, assess its condition, and prepare it the right way for that material, rather than applying a one-size approach that works on one kind of base and fails on another.

When the joists, not the subfloor, are the problem

Sometimes a bouncy or sloping floor is not the subfloor at all, it is the framing underneath. In older Medford homes, joists can sag, twist, or sit on settled supports after a hundred years, and no amount of surface leveling fixes a structural issue.

When we find that the real problem is below the subfloor, we say so honestly rather than pouring leveling compound over a moving base. Depending on what we find, that may mean sistering a joist, adding support, or bringing in the right trade before flooring goes down.

This is exactly why the subfloor assessment matters: catching a structural issue before the finish floor goes on saves you from installing a beautiful floor over a base that will keep moving. We would rather flag it up front than have your floor telegraph the problem later.

Why proper prep saves money in the long run

Skipping or shortcutting subfloor prep is the most tempting way to save on a flooring job and the most expensive one over time. The savings are real on day one and vanish the first time the floor squeaks, gaps, or has to come up.

A floor installed over a poor base fails early, and fixing it means tearing up and reinstalling the finish floor you already paid for, plus finally doing the subfloor work that should have happened first. You pay for the prep either way; doing it up front means paying once.

We build the honest prep into the documented estimate so there are no surprises, and so the floor you invest in actually delivers the decades of service it should.

Underlayment's role over the subfloor

The subfloor and the underlayment work as a team, and once the base is flat and sound, the right underlayment finishes the job of preparing for the finish floor. It is easy to overlook and it matters for how the floor feels and sounds.

Underlayment cushions floating floors, dampens sound, smooths out the last minor imperfections, and, over concrete or below grade, carries the moisture barrier that protects the finish floor. The right choice depends on both the subfloor and the floor going on top, which is why we select it as part of the prep rather than grabbing a generic roll.

In multi-family Medford buildings, a good acoustic underlayment over a properly prepped subfloor also cuts the sound transfer between units, which is often written into the building's requirements and always appreciated by the neighbors below.

The base you never see is the one that matters most

It is easy to spend all your attention on the visible floor, the species, the color, the pattern, and none on the subfloor, because no one ever sees it. Yet the base is what determines whether that beautiful floor stays beautiful or starts squeaking, gapping, and cupping within a year.

Every dollar and hour spent getting the subfloor flat, sound, and dry is invisible on the finished job and decisive on how it performs for the next twenty years. It is the least glamorous and most important part of any flooring project, and it is where cutting corners hurts most.

That is why we treat the subfloor as the foundation of the whole job in every Medford home, not a step to rush past. Get the base right and the floor on top simply works, quietly, flatly, for decades.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my subfloor need leveling before a new floor?
Because any floor on top is only as flat and quiet as the base. An uneven subfloor causes flex, gaps, squeaks, and, with floating floors, a hollow feel. Leveling first is what makes the finished floor solid and long-lasting.
How do you level a subfloor?
Depending on the situation, with self-leveling compound to fill dips, shimming or sanding high spots, or repairing and replacing sections. Over a slab we may also address moisture. We choose the method after inspecting the base.
Can you fix squeaks coming from the subfloor?
Yes. Many squeaks come from a subfloor moving against the joists or loose panels. We secure the base as part of the prep, so the finished floor is quiet.
Is subfloor work part of my flooring estimate?
Usually yes. When we install a floor we inspect the base and include any needed prep in the documented estimate, so there are no surprises once the old floor comes up.
Do I always need subfloor work before a new floor?
Not always, but we always check. If the base is already flat, sound, and dry, we say so. When it needs leveling or repair, doing it first is what makes the finished floor last, and it goes in your documented estimate.
How long does self-leveling compound take to cure?
It varies by product and depth, but it needs to cure before flooring can go on top. We schedule the install around the cure time so the finished floor sits on a fully set, flat base.
